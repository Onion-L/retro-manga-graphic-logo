# Retro Manga Graphic Logo

Create a **single, original 1:1 character logo** using a high-contrast
retro Japanese manga graphic language.

The goal is not to reproduce a reference character. Extract and preserve
the **visual system**—black/white structure, shape language, negative
space, line weight, simplification, and print-inspired treatment—while
designing a new character.

## Core visual identity

Always prioritize these characteristics:

- Pure black and pure white only.
- No grayscale.
- No gradients.
- No color.
- Extreme black/white contrast.
- Large, bold solid-black graphic shapes.
- Strong, readable silhouette.
- Deliberate negative space.
- White areas function as solid-white cutouts from black forms, not transparent holes.
- Sharp, graphic white highlights rather than soft lighting.
- Clean, bold, vector-like contours.
- Flat graphic geometry.
- Minimal but expressive facial features.
- Retro Japanese manga / shōjo manga influence.
- Vintage screen-print and linocut-inspired shape language.
- Fully opaque solid white background (#FFFFFF).
- No transparency or alpha channel.
- Square 1:1 composition.
- Single character as the primary subject.
- Logo-first, not illustration-first.

## Composition

Default to a tightly cropped portrait or bust:

- Head and shoulders are preferred.
- Character should occupy most of the square.
- Keep the silhouette close to the edges without feeling cramped.
- Use a simple, uncluttered, fully opaque white background.
- Avoid decorative backgrounds, scenery, text, badges, borders, or extra
  objects unless explicitly requested.
- A three-quarter view, side profile, or slightly turned pose is preferred
  over a generic front-facing portrait.
- The silhouette should remain recognizable when reduced to a small icon.

## Character design

Create an **original character** with deliberately different identifying
features from any reference.

Vary:

- Face shape
- Hairstyle
- Hair length
- Bangs
- Eye shape
- Eyebrows
- Expression
- Head angle
- Pose
- Accessories
- Clothing
- Silhouette

Do not copy a reference character's exact face, hairstyle, clothing, pose,
proportions, or composition.

For human characters, favor distinctive graphic features over realistic
anatomy. A character can be attractive, cute, cool, strange, elegant,
nerdy, mysterious, or playful depending on the requested personality.

## Shape language

Build the character from a small number of strong visual masses.

### Hair

Hair is often one of the largest black masses.

Use:

- Large unified black shapes
- Bold bangs
- Chunky locks
- Sharp or rhythmic white cutouts
- A few deliberate white highlight shapes

Avoid rendering individual hairs or realistic strands.

### Face

The face should mostly be represented by white negative space.

Use a restrained set of black marks for:

- Eyes
- Eyelashes
- Eyebrows
- Nose
- Mouth
- Small facial accents

Do not over-render skin texture or facial shading.

### Eyes

Eyes may retain a retro manga influence:

- Expressive
- Graphic
- Clearly readable
- Slightly exaggerated

But keep them integrated into the overall logo language rather than
turning the result into modern detailed anime art.

### Clothing and accessories

Simplify clothing into large graphic masses.

Accessories such as:

- Glasses
- Headphones
- Earrings
- Hair clips
- Hats
- Collars

can be used as strong identity elements, but should remain visually
simple.

Do not add arbitrary letters, logos, symbols, or text to clothing unless
explicitly requested.

## Negative space

Negative space is a defining feature.

Think of the design as:

> black shapes first, white cutouts second.

Use solid white shapes to carve:

- Face shapes
- Hair highlights
- Ear/headphone separation
- Clothing separation
- Eye reflections
- Small structural gaps

The white areas should be solid white and feel intentional and graphic,
not like accidental empty space or transparent holes.

## Print-inspired treatment

Use the visual language of:

- Vintage Japanese manga printing
- Screen printing
- Linocut
- Woodcut-inspired graphic forms

However, keep the final artwork **clean and polished**.

Do not introduce:

- Paper grain
- Dirt
- Aging artifacts
- Halftone noise
- Heavy ink bleed
- Photographic texture

unless specifically requested.

The printmaking influence should come primarily from the **shape
construction and contrast**, not from adding surface noise.

## Logo requirements

The result must read as a logo.

Prioritize:

1. Silhouette
2. Black/white balance
3. Negative space
4. Distinctive character features
5. Small-size readability
6. Visual simplicity

The logo should still be recognizable at approximately **32 × 32 px**.

When forced to choose between detail and recognizability, remove detail.

## Avoid

Do not produce:

- Full-color artwork
- Grayscale shading
- Gradients
- Soft airbrush rendering
- Photorealism
- Painterly rendering
- Detailed backgrounds
- Realistic skin texture
- Excessive cross-hatching
- Dense comic panels
- Speech bubbles
- Text
- Watermarks
- Generic clip-art appearance
- Overly detailed modern anime illustration
- A direct copy of the reference character
- Transparent backgrounds, alpha channels, checkerboard backgrounds, or
  transparent PNG output

## Prompt construction

When generating, structure the request internally as:

**SUBJECT** > Who or what is the character?

**PERSONALITY** > What emotional impression should the character
communicate?

**DISTINCTIVE FEATURES** > Hair, eyes, glasses, headphones, clothing,
accessories, etc.

**POSE / VIEW** > Three-quarter view, side profile, looking over shoulder,
front-facing, etc.

**STYLE** > Apply the complete Retro Manga Graphic Logo style defined
above.

**COMPOSITION** > Single character, tightly cropped, fully opaque white
background (#FFFFFF), no transparency or alpha channel, 1:1 square.

**ORIGINALITY** > Create an original character. Do not reproduce the
reference character's identity, face, hairstyle, pose, clothing, or
composition.

## Example prompt

> Create a single 1:1 logo of an original geek girl. She has short wavy
> hair, oversized round glasses, large headphones, and a slightly curious
> expression. Use pure black and pure white only, no grayscale or
> gradients. Build the character from large bold black graphic shapes
> with strong negative space and sharp white cutout highlights. Use a
> retro Japanese manga and vintage screen-print / linocut-inspired visual
> language, with clean vector-like contours and minimal expressive facial
> details. Tight head-and-shoulders crop, three-quarter view, white
> fully opaque white background (#FFFFFF), no transparency or alpha channel,
> strong iconic silhouette, logo-first design, recognizable at 32 × 32 px.
> Do not copy any existing character or reference image; create
> a clearly original face, hairstyle, pose, and silhouette.

## Quality check

Before finalizing, verify:

- Is the background pure white and fully opaque?
- Are all cutouts and highlights solid white rather than transparent?
- Is there any transparency, alpha channel, or checkerboard background?
- Is the artwork strictly black and white?
- Is there any unintended gray or gradient?
- Is there one clear primary character?
- Does the silhouette read immediately?
- Are the black shapes large and intentional?
- Is negative space doing meaningful work?
- Does it look like a logo rather than a detailed illustration?
- Is the character clearly original?
- Would the design remain recognizable when reduced to a small icon?
